\section{What an upper-bound route has to do}

An upper bound is a construction: exhibit $100$ centres $c_1,\dots,c_{100}$ and a rational $U$, and
prove $\max_{x\in D}\min_i\|x-c_i\|\le U$. The mathematical content is therefore always the same
two-step reduction
\[
\underbrace{D\subseteq\bigcup_i B(c_i,U)}_{\text{continuous statement}}
\;\Longleftarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{finite list of exact box/leaf checks}}_{\text{machine decision}} ,
\]
and the whole difficulty is in the implication. Three different finite reductions are used in this
programme, and they are worth separating because they have different strengths.

\subsection{Reduction A: quadtree / box subdivision with an exact distance criterion}

Cover $D$ by axis-parallel boxes. For a box $X$ and a centre $c$, an interval evaluation of
$\|x-c\|^2$ over $X$ gives $[\ell(X,c),u(X,c)]$. The box is certified if
$\min_i \ell(X,c_i)\le U^2$ with the minimum computed exactly; otherwise subdivide. A leaf whose
interval cannot be decided is \emph{unresolved} and must be reported, never rounded into
``covered''. The initial continuous-coverage certificate for the historical configuration is of
this type: $R=153/1250$, $22\,804$ leaves, depth $16$, $0$ unresolved, $21/21$ checks
(\texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}coverage\_\allowbreak{}n100.json}). An independent checker re-derives the criterion from
integer corner comparisons and shares no geometric function with the generator; it rejects three
deliberately corrupted cases (\texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}independent\_\allowbreak{}coverage\_\allowbreak{}n100.json}). A control
battery of $12$ positive and negative cases passes
(\texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}coverage\_\allowbreak{}controls\_\allowbreak{}n100.json}). All of these carry
\texttt{machine\_\allowbreak{}verified\_\allowbreak{}geometry = false}: the reduction from ``boxes certified'' to ``$D$ is
covered'' is analytic.

\subsection{Reduction B: lattice-cell reduction (exact, with a finite enumeration)}

For a lattice construction one can do much better than a quadtree: the cells tile the plane, so
coverage of $D$ reduces to a finite exact check over the finitely many lattice sites whose cells
can meet $D$. This is the method of \S\ref{sec:lattice}, and it produces a certificate whose
decisions are integer/Fraction only.

\subsection{Reduction C: replay of an external configuration}

When the configuration is given by an external package, the honest upper-bound route is a
byte-faithful replay of that package's own checker plus independent cross-checks. This is the
method of \S\ref{sec:external}.

\section{The triangular-lattice $97{+}3$ configuration}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{The configuration}

Work first in \emph{normalized} coordinates where nearest lattice neighbours are at distance $1$.
A lattice site is a pair $(i,j)\in\Z^2$ with real position
\[
x(i,j)=(u,\sqrt3\,v),\qquad u=i+\tfrac j2,\qquad v=\tfrac j2,
\qquad
\|x(i,j)\|^2=i^2+ij+j^2 .
\]
The hexagonal Voronoi cell of a site is the translate of the hexagon $H$ with vertices
$(0,\pm\frac13)$, $(\pm\frac12,\pm\frac16)$ in $(u,v)$ coordinates, whose circumradius is
$1/\sqrt3$.

\begin{construction}[The $97{+}3$ configuration]\label{con:lattice}
Take all sites with $i^2+ij+j^2<28$ (there are $97$ of them), plus the three norm-$28$ sites
\[
(-6,4),\quad(-2,-4),\quad(2,4).
\]
With the scaling
\[
a=\sqrt{3/67},\qquad R=1/a=\sqrt{67/3},\qquad \alpha=1/\sqrt{67},
\]
the $100$ closed disks of radius $\alpha$ about the scaled centres $a\,x(i,j)$ cover $D$, and
$\alpha$ is \emph{exactly} the covering radius of this centre configuration.
\end{construction}

\begin{proposition}[Exact covering radius of the configuration]\label{prop:lattice-alpha}
$\alpha=1/\sqrt{67}$ is the least radius at which the $100$ centres of Construction~\ref{con:lattice}
cover $D$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
The upper half is the cell check described below. For the lower half, consider the interior lattice
hole $q=(u,v)=(\tfrac12,\tfrac16)$, i.e.\ the centre of a hexagon surrounded by six sites. Its
normalized norm is $\|q\|^2=u^2+3v^2=\tfrac14+\tfrac1{12}=\tfrac13$, and its distance to the nearest
lattice site is the hexagon circumradius $1/\sqrt3$. After scaling by $a$, the nearest retained
centre is at distance $a/\sqrt3=1/\sqrt{67}=\alpha$, while $\|a q\|=a/\sqrt3=\alpha<1$, so $aq$ is a
point of $D$ at distance exactly $\alpha$ from every centre. Hence no smaller radius covers $D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The three norm-$28$ sites are \emph{redundant} for coverage: the $97$-site configuration already
covers $D$ with the same radius, and the certificate records the construction order
($97$ sites, then three extra sites). Redundancy does not prove that a smaller radius is achievable
with $100$ disks; it only shows that this particular configuration does not use its budget.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The exact cell argument}

The certificate \texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}lattice97\_\allowbreak{}upperbound\_\allowbreak{}n100.json} (schema
\texttt{n100-\allowbreak{}lattice97-\allowbreak{}upperbound-\allowbreak{}v1}) reduces continuous coverage to a finite exact check by four
steps, each of which is an integer or \texttt{Fraction} decision:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Cell containment.} Every point of the Voronoi cell of a site $p$ is within
$1/\sqrt3$ of $p$ (circumradius of $H$).
\item \textbf{Finite site list.} A site whose cell meets the closed disk of radius $R=\sqrt{67/3}$
satisfies $\|p\|^2<29$, hence $\|p\|^2\le28$, hence $|i|,|j|\le7$. The enumeration box used is
$|i|,|j|\le24$, generously larger than the sufficient box $7$.
\item \textbf{Exact minimum at a unique vertex.} For every site $p\notin C$ with $\|p\|^2\le28$,
the minimum of $\|x\|^2$ over the cell is exactly $R^2=67/3$, attained at a unique vertex $v_p$;
this is certified by the supporting-plane inequality $\langle v_p,\,w-v_p\rangle\ge0$ over all six
vertices $w$ of the cell.
\item \textbf{Exterior shell.} Sites with $\|p\|^2\ge29$ have cell distance
$\ge\sqrt{29}-1/\sqrt3>R$, certified by the pure integer inequality $3(29-22)^2>4\cdot29$, i.e.\
$7>2\sqrt{29/3}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Algebraic data of the candidate radius}

\[
\alpha=\frac1{\sqrt{67}},\qquad P(t)=67t^2-1 \ \ (\text{coefficients } [67,0,-1]),
\]
$P$ is primitive with positive leading coefficient; $67$ is not a square, so $P$ is irreducible in
$\Q[t]$; and
\[
\frac{1221694443}{10^{10}}<\alpha<\frac{1221694444}{10^{10}}
\]
is a rational isolating interval. The endpoint signs are decided by integer square comparison, and
$P$ is strictly increasing on the positive half-line ($P'(t)=134t>0$), so the interval contains
exactly one root. This is the minimal polynomial of \emph{this configuration's} covering radius ---
it is \emph{not} claimed to be the minimal polynomial of $\rD(100)$, because the lower bounds of
Part III/IV do not meet $\alpha$.

\subsection{Negative controls in the certificate}

The lattice certificate's thirteen negative controls must all be rejected, and are: a scaled-up
target disk (precondition only, explicitly \emph{not} an uncovered witness for the delivered claim);
an explicit uncovered point for the enlarged-disk variant; a shrunk covering radius; an enlarged
covering radius; a wrong metric; a wrong/rotated hexagon; an inflated hexagon; a truncated neighbour
search; a misattributed neighbour; two removed tangency centres; the $97$-centre configuration
submitted as a ``$100$-point'' claim; a single shell centre removed (coverage still holds, so only the
cardinality is rejected); and a removed interior centre. An independent implementation
({\footnotesize\texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}independent\_\allowbreak{}lattice97\_\allowbreak{}upperbound\_\allowbreak{}n100.json}}, \texttt{all\_\allowbreak{}passed = true},
\texttt{coverage\_\allowbreak{}confirmed = true}) reproduces the claim with a different code path.

\subsection{Why the cooperative track stopped improving}

The algebraic diagnostics are as important as the bounds. At every stage there was exactly one
tight hole (threshold $10^{-9}$), with several near-tight holes participating in a finite band
model. The polish run still recorded $115$ strict improvements when it hit its $4\times30$-round
cap, so the end of the descent had not been reached: the local system has \emph{no} KKT or
neighbourhood-optimality certificate. A stationarity diagnostic ran in $1.3$ s and reported: a
$10^{-6}$ near-active band with $123$ contacts, but only $2$ contacts at $10^{-7}$ and $1$ at
$10^{-9}$; $114$ interior gradient rows of floating-point rank $114$ with nullspace dimension $86$;
and no non-negative KKT multiplier LP found at the tested tolerances. A contact-balance/SQP unit
returned \texttt{NO\_\allowbreak{}IMPROVEMENT}: in $60$ s it lowered the radius by about $1.25\times10^{-9}$,
below the acceptance threshold, so no new candidate was saved and no upper bound was updated.
Smooth-max directions mostly increased the true radius and were rejected by the true evaluator.
All of this is finite floating-point diagnosis: it neither proves that no other equilibrium branch
exists nor that the candidate is ``non-algebraizable''.

\section{What the upper bounds do \emph{not} give}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item They do not identify $\rD(100)$: the accepted interval has width $0.0022$ in absolute terms.
\item They do not prove that the lattice configuration, the cooperative candidate, or the external
configuration is optimal or locally optimal (except for the one isolated KKT root, where
\emph{strict local} minimality of the fixed contact graph is certified --- a local statement about
one graph).
\item They do not yield the minimal polynomial of $\rD(100)$. What exists is the minimal polynomial
$67t^2-1$ of the covering radius of one explicit configuration, which is a different object.
\item The three redundant lattice disks show that this configuration does not saturate its budget;
that is a hint, not a proof, that a smaller radius might be reachable with $100$ disks.
\end{itemize}
