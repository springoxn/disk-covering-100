\section{The logical skeleton}

Everything in this Part has the following shape.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]
\item Fix a rational \emph{candidate radius} $R$ and suppose, for contradiction, that $100$ closed
disks of radius $R$ cover $D$.
\item \textbf{Normalize} every centre at that radius (same-radius normalization) and rebuild the
ownership relation, the number of effectively used disks, and the common triangulation. Nothing from
a previous radius may be reused.
\item \textbf{Count.} Derive necessary conditions that exclude all configurations with at most
$m-1$ effectively used disks, and, for $m=100$, restrict the number $B$ of boundary arcs to a finite
list.
\item \textbf{Split into branches.} The surviving $(m,B)$ pairs are partitioned into six mutually
exclusive branches $B31$, $B32$, $B33$, $B34$, $B35$, $B{\ge}36$.
\item \textbf{Close each branch} by an area/curvature budget: an exact rational inequality that
contradicts coverage.
\item \textbf{Combine.} A combiner checks that every branch is closed at the same radius, that
budgets are spent once, and that any branch closed at a \emph{larger} radius is inherited through
radius monotonicity.
\end{enumerate}

The last point is a theorem, not a convention:

\begin{lemma}[Radius monotonicity]\label{lem:monotone}
If no covering by $100$ disks of radius $R'$ exists, then no covering by $100$ disks of radius $R<R'$
exists.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A covering at radius $R$ is a covering at radius $R'$ after enlarging every disk, since
$B(c,R)\subseteq B(c,R')$.
\end{proof}

Hence a branch certificate established at $R'=2831/25000$ is available at any $R\le R'$, provided
the combiner records the inheritance explicitly (grade D evidence). The tail branch is \emph{not}
inherited in this way in the current chain: it is replayed directly at $R_\star=22647/200000$.

\section{Normalization at a fixed radius}

\subsection{The radial map}

For a candidate radius $R$, define the radial map
\[
d'=\frac{1-R^2}{d},
\]
where $d$ is the distance from the origin to a centre, and let $u$ be the unit vector of that centre.
The identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radial-identity}
\|z-d'u\|^2-R^2=\frac{d'}{d}\bigl(\|z-du\|^2-R^2\bigr)+\Bigl(1-\frac{d'}{d}\Bigr)\bigl(\|z\|^2-1\bigr)
\end{equation}
is asserted exactly by CAS in the certificates. Since $d'/d\le1$ and $\|z\|^2-1\le0$ for
$z\in D$, every point already covered at radius $R$ remains covered after the map. The normalization
is applied to \emph{all} centres \emph{before} the counting branch is taken; ownership, the number of
effective disks and the common triangulation are rebuilt from scratch. This is the reason the
engine cannot be reused at a new radius by editing one constant: the diagram itself changes.

The recurring mechanism in every branch is a \emph{budget}: the boundary arc length available at
radius $R$ must pay for interior area, for boundary ``marked'' triangles, for ear and bad-face
deficits, and for one hinge or variance payment. Two structural rules are enforced throughout:
(i) a physical edge shared by two faces is paid \emph{once}, and opposite half-edge terms cancel
exactly; (ii) a scalar deficit or a local gain may not be spent in two different rows. The
certificates record these as explicit fields (\texttt{physical\_\allowbreak{}edge\_\allowbreak{}pairs},
\texttt{hinge\_\allowbreak{}payment = 0}, \texttt{variance\_\allowbreak{}payment = 0}, budget-review strings).

\section{The tail branch $B\ge36$}

\subsection{The capped-pair relaxation}

For the high boundary counts the engine changes character. Instead of a global face ledger it
constructs a \emph{local pair loss} for each adjacent pair of boundary cells. In the absorbed
formulation a pair term has the shape
\[
\Phi(a,b)=\lambda\,\|c_a-c_b\|^2+\frac{H(a)+H(b)}{2}-\frac{\mu\,(\arcsin s_a+\arcsin s_b)}{2},
\]
where $a,b$ are adjacent half-arc angles, $c_a,c_b$ the corresponding centres, $H$ a geometric loss
built from the arc segment and the distance from the centre to the arc chord, and
$\lambda,\mu$ penalty and slope coefficients. When the local terms are summed around the ring, the
exact identity
\[
\sum_i\frac{a_i+a_{i+1}}2=\sum_i a_i=\pi
\]
is used --- not a Jensen-type ``equal arcs'' conjecture. Each local pair upper bound is then fed into
a total area budget, and two scalar checks are performed: the margin at $B=36$ must be strictly
positive, and the gain per additional boundary cell must be strictly positive, which extends the
exclusion to every $B\ge36$.

The scalar budget actually implemented by the certificate scripts is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-budget}
\underbrace{(1-\mu)\,\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}}_{\text{available arc budget}}
\;-\;\underbrace{\bigl((2m-2-B)\,A+B\,K\bigr)R^2}_{\text{interior and marked triangle area}}
\;-\;\underbrace{B\,L}_{\text{intercept}}\;>\;0,
\qquad
\underbrace{(A-K)R^2-L}_{\text{gain per boundary cell}}\;>\;0,
\end{equation}
with $m=100$, $A=129903810568/10^{11}$ (an outward rational enclosure of $3\sqrt3/4$) and $K$ the
marked-triangle constant. Substituting the frozen constants reproduces the stored margins exactly,
which is how the two certificate fields below were checked:
\[
\text{at }R=\tfrac{113237}{10^6},\ L=\tfrac{75097}{10^7}:\quad
\text{margin}=4.906445222464608\times10^{-7},
\]
\[
\text{gain}=5.1549192230158705\times10^{-3},
\]
\[
\text{at }R=\tfrac{22647}{200000},\ L=\tfrac{939}{125000}:\quad
\text{margin}=1.8087101250224213\times10^{-5},
\]
\[
\text{gain}=5.152171860143971\times10^{-3}.
\]
The underlying geometric derivation of \eqref{eq:scalar-budget} lives in the absorbed third-party
package; what this project certifies is the exact arithmetic of the budget, the completeness of the
rational square-domain subdivision described next, and the negative controls.

\subsection{Why the tail does not extend to $0.11324$}

At $R=2831/25000$ the \emph{old} fixed coefficients give a total budget of about $-0.000160907$: the
tail cannot close at $0.11324$ even if its local supports remain valid. Holding $\mu$ and $\lambda$
fixed, the intercept would have to be strictly below about $0.007505530351154887$; the trial
$L=0.007505$ gave a conditional budget of about $+0.0000190926$, but the complete two-dimensional
local support was never verified (a $250\,000$-box run accepted $124\,491$ leaves, left $17$ pending
stack items and returned \texttt{UNDECIDED}).

The obstruction is exact, not numerical. With the old constants, at the rational point
\[
x=y=\frac{9921033}{12500000}
\]
the pair relaxation satisfies
\[
\frac{3754996055077}{500000000000000}\ \le\ \text{pair}\ \le\ \frac{3754996055079}{500000000000000},
\]
so its lower endpoint exceeds the old intercept $1501/200000$ by
\[
\frac{2496055077}{500000000000000}\approx4.99211\times10^{-6},
\]
and it also exceeds the largest intercept that the $B=36$ area budget can pay for,
\[
\frac{27019909263463328303182469778967931605940457120574226219}
{3600000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000},
\]
by
\[
\frac{16062333091071696817530221032068394059542879425773781}
{3600000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000000}.
\]
Consequently the deficit cannot be repaired by increasing a box budget or by raising the intercept:
the latter makes the $B=36$ budget negative. A later exact diagnostic showed that both support terms
vanish at the obstruction point, so refining the support boxes alone is mathematically insufficient
as well. The obstruction certificate carries

\texttt{full\_\allowbreak{}B36plus\_\allowbreak{}closed = false}, \texttt{global\_\allowbreak{}bound\_\allowbreak{}improved = false}, and its scope
string states explicitly that this is not a covering configuration and not a counterexample to the
target lower bound.
