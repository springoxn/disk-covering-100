\section{Statement of the problem}

\begin{definition}[Covering radius and the extremal quantity]
Let $D=\{x\in\R^2:\|x\|_2\le1\}$. For a centre tuple $C=(c_1,\dots,c_{100})\in(\R^2)^{100}$ put
\[
R_D(C)=\max_{x\in D}\ \min_{1\le i\le 100}\|x-c_i\|_2,
\qquad
\rD(100)=\inf_{C\in(\R^2)^{100}}R_D(C).
\]
The covering disks are \emph{closed}; they may overlap and may extend beyond $D$; the centres are
not restricted to $D$. The task of the programme is to determine $\rD(100)$ together with an
explicit minimal polynomial of it, an explicit configuration attaining it, a coverage proof and a
global optimality proof, all backed by reproducible computer-assisted material.
\end{definition}

The five formal requirements are, in the order in which they constrain the work:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Minimal polynomial and isolation.} List every coefficient of the primitive integer
polynomial $P$ of the optimal radius, prove irreducibility over $\Q$, and give rationals $a<b$ with
$P(a)P(b)\ne0$ and exactly one root of $P$ in $(a,b)$.
\item \textbf{Explicit configuration.} A definite ordered $100$-tuple of centres given by exact
algebraic data.
\item \textbf{Coverage.} A rigorous proof that every $x\in D$ is within $\alpha$ of some centre; if
the continuous statement is reduced to finitely many checks, the reduction itself must be proved
sufficient.
\item \textbf{Global optimality.} $R_D(C)\ge\alpha$ for \emph{every} $C$, with no unproved
restriction on symmetry, contact structure or combinatorial type, and with degeneracies
(coincident centres, redundant disks, collinear centres, tangential contacts) handled explicitly.
\item \textbf{Computer-assisted proof.} Complete code, inputs, certificates, environment and actual
verification results, with an explanation of why those results imply the mathematical statements.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why the problem is hard}

\subsection{Continuous, non-convex, combinatorially unstable}

The objective $C\mapsto R_D(C)$ is a maximum of minima of distances: continuous, piecewise smooth,
and non-convex. Its minimizers are not characterized by a single smooth system, because the
combinatorial type of the induced decomposition changes with $r$: which centre owns a boundary arc,
which triples of centres meet at a Voronoi vertex, and how many arcs the boundary carries are all
functions of the configuration. Consequently a numerical optimizer can only ever produce an upper
bound. A lower bound must exclude \emph{all} configurations, and the standard way to do that is to
pass to a finite combinatorial catalogue of possible decompositions and then exclude each catalogue
entry by exact inequalities.

\subsection{Symmetry is not a necessary condition for optimality}

The classical counterexample is $n=5$: the fully symmetric arrangement gives
$r=\varphi-1=0.6180340\ldots$, whereas the true optimum is $0.6093829\ldots$, attained by a
configuration with a single mirror axis. This is Connelly's question (must an optimal covering
configuration have $n$-fold rotational symmetry?), answered negatively for $n=5$ and affirmatively
for $n=3$ by Csik\'os. For the present programme the practical consequence is blunt: one may
\emph{not} restrict to a symmetric family and call the result $\rD(100)$.

\subsection{Enumeration is not viable at $n=100$}

The mature paradigm for rigorous computer-assisted optimality proofs is
\emph{combinatorial classification plus interval Newton / branch-and-bound}. Nurmela and
\"Osterg{\aa}rd pushed the rigorous optimality of equal-circle \emph{packings} in a square to
$n\le27$; their own account records that the initial combinatorial count grows rapidly, that
$23\le n\le26$ cost CPU-months, and that $n=27$ required a change of algorithm. The present
programme's estimate for $n=100$ is consistent with this: the orbit count of covering
configurations is astronomical. Enumeration and classification are therefore used here only as
\emph{local} devices inside a branch, never as the global method.

Three facts from that snapshot shape everything below. First, the asymptotic density statement is
rigorous but asymptotic: $\lim_{\eps\to0^+}\eps^2N(\eps)=2\pi/(3\sqrt3)$, i.e.\ covering density
$\delta=2\pi/(3\sqrt3)=1.2091996\ldots$ (Kershner 1939; Verblunsky 1949). Converting an asymptotic
statement into a finite-$n$ bound requires a boundary correction term, and that correction is
precisely where the difficulty sits. Second, the unique successful ``symmetry-forcing'' paradigm
(Fejes T\'oth's proof for $n=8,9,10$) works only in the structure ``one central disk plus one
ring''; $n=25$ (centre plus three octagonal rings) and $n=100$ (many rings) have no such theorem.
Third, the covering-density inequality of L.~Fejes T\'oth with a perimeter term, and its packing
dual (Oler/Thue--Groemer, $\frac{1}{2\sqrt3}A(C)+\frac14P(C)+1\ge n$), are the literature
prototypes of the ``cell-by-cell plus boundary correction'' route --- which Part III shows is
\emph{not} strong enough here, and Part IV replaces by a counting-plus-budget engine.

\section{Conventions and normalizations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Closed everything.} Disks, the unit disk, and all rational intervals are closed.
A closed interval $[a,b]$ used as an outer enclosure means the true value lies in $[a,b]$; a
closed radius equality is retained rather than discarded.
\item \textbf{Decision layer.} Every accepted inequality is decided by integer, \texttt{Fraction},
or outward-rounded interval arithmetic. Floating point appears only in the discovery layer: Newton
roots, candidate configurations, factor sieving, LLL/PSLQ candidate relations. A discovery result
must be re-decided exactly before it can enter a certificate.
\item \textbf{Projection.} If $c\notin D$, let $\Pi(c)$ be its projection onto the convex set $D$.
Since $\Pi$ is non-expansive, $B(\Pi(c),r)\supseteq B(c,r)\cap D$; replacing every centre by its
projection preserves both coverage and radius. One may therefore assume $\|c_i\|\le1$. This does
\emph{not} place the centre inside the inscribed polygon $P$ used in Part III, and one of the
geometric lemmas there exists precisely to remove that older assumption.
\item \textbf{Deduplication.} If $c_i=c_j$ for $i\ne j$, delete one disk: coverage and radius are
unchanged and the centre set shrinks. After deduplication all centres are distinct and every
Voronoi cell has positive area.
\item \textbf{Non-overwriting outputs.} Certificate scripts refuse to write over an existing JSON
target. Re-running a stage therefore requires a fresh output path, which is how the repository
distinguishes ``this certificate was produced by this run'' from ``a file with this name exists''.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The evidence discipline}

\subsection{Five layers that must not be conflated}

The programme's own integrated report separates five layers of verification, and insists that an
earlier layer never substitutes for a later one:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.26\textwidth}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.62\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Layer & What it establishes \\
\midrule
1. file existence / hash & which bytes this run actually consumed \\
2. metadata / entry verification & rejection of duplicate nodes, wrong domains, unauthorized
entries, and unjustified budget addition \\
3. exact arithmetic replay & recomputation of the necessary conditions and certificates for a
\emph{given} domain \\
4. analytic / geometric bridge & that every genuine covering configuration falls into that domain,
i.e.\ that the necessary constraints really hold \\
5. global coverage & that all branches, graphs and labellings are enumerated or enclosed \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{\texttt{PASS} means ``passed within the scope field''}

A stored \texttt{PASS} asserts exactly what the certificate's \texttt{scope} field says, no more.
Three concrete incidents from this programme make the point:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{An independent script copied the same error.} In stage 45/46 a boundary-annulus
certificate used a wrong inner-face filter, producing an empty pending set; the ``globally
no-gain'' acceptance was voided. The corrected run actually checked $32$ boundary-owning faces,
$4776$ configurations and $152\,832$ exact annulus comparisons, and added $0$ exclusions. The
lesson recorded in the state file is that the independent script had duplicated the same filter
error, so \emph{exit code 0 is not sufficient evidence that the mathematical coverage domain is
right}.
\item \textbf{A \texttt{PASS} on a different radius.} The $B31$ certificate was established at
$R=2831/25000$; it is usable at a smaller radius only through radius monotonicity, and the combiner
must record that inheritance explicitly. A \texttt{PASS} at one radius is not a \texttt{PASS} at
another.
\item \textbf{A \texttt{PASS\_\allowbreak{}PREMISES}.} The $B35$ diagnostic certificate returns
\texttt{status = PASS\_\allowbreak{}PREMISES} with \texttt{B35\_\allowbreak{}closed = false}: all the premises were verified
and the branch was \emph{not} closed. This state word exists so that such a run can never be read
as a closure.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Negative controls are part of the certificate}

Almost every certificate script ships destruction tests that must be rejected: a target disk scaled
up, an explicit uncovered point for an enlarged-disk variant, a shrunk covering radius, an enlarged
covering radius, a wrong metric, a rotated or inflated Voronoi hexagon, a truncated neighbour
search, a misattributed neighbour, removed tangency centres, a removed interior centre, a
$97$-centre configuration submitted as a $100$-point claim, a broken edge pairing, a broken face
permutation, a support declared inactive, an assertion disabled with \texttt{python -\allowbreak{}O}, and
several deliberately corrupted rational payloads. A run whose negative controls are not all
rejected is not accepted. Two entry points additionally refuse to run under \texttt{python -\allowbreak{}O} and
report \emph{Assertions must remain enabled}.

\subsection{What must never be inferred}

The evidence ledger states the prohibitions explicitly, and they are the reason this document
keeps its failures visible:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item a passed local box is not a global lower bound;
\item an unblocked obstruction point is not a closed branch;
\item an existing configuration is not an optimality proof;
\item a numerical ``passed'' print is not a proof;
\item checking finitely many sample points, the boundary, or a numerical image is not a proof that
the whole closed unit disk is covered.
\end{itemize}
