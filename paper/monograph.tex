\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We study $r_{D}(100)$, the least radius $r$ such that $100$ congruent closed disks of radius $r$
cover the closed unit disk $D$. This monograph is a systematic, route-by-route account of the
whole research programme: every construction that was tried, every relaxation that was closed,
and every route that was falsified or left undecided. The currently accepted, machine-replayable
statement is
\[
\boxed{\;0.113235\;<\;\rD(100)\;\le\;0.1154469548855829\;}
\]
The lower bound is a six-branch exclusion certificate at the rational radius
$R=22647/200000$: $B31$--$B35$ are inherited by radius monotonicity from certificates at
$R=2831/25000=0.11324$, while the tail branch $B\ge 36$ is replayed directly at $R$ over
$1\,841\,089$ rational boxes. The upper bound is an explicitly replayed $100$-centre
configuration. Neither statement identifies the exact optimum. The nearby target
$0.11324$ remains open: with the frozen capped-pair constants the branch $B=36$ carries an
exact rational obstruction point at which the local pair relaxation exceeds the intercept that
the $B=36$ area budget can pay for. We explain why that obstruction cannot be removed by
increasing a box budget or raising an intercept, and we record the undecided window
$(0.113235,\,0.11324]$.

Every quantitative claim below is tied to a machine-readable artifact in \texttt{verify/out/}.
The document deliberately keeps its failures: a research record that only contains successes
cannot tell the next reader which relaxations are too weak.
\end{abstract}
