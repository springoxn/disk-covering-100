\noindent
This Part is the systematic inventory the rest of the document prepares for. Each row gives the
route, its construction, its status and its evidence. Status vocabulary:
\textbf{closed} (a strict certificate exists inside its declared domain --- possibly a conditional
sub-branch), \textbf{partial} (genuine strict exclusions covering only part of the declared domain),
\textbf{diagnostic} (measurement, audit or bottleneck only), \textbf{falsified} (the claim or its
premise was explicitly refuted or a counterexample exists), \textbf{undecided} (ran to completion
with zero new exclusions), \textbf{interface} (\texttt{PASS} means contract registration or replay
consistency, \emph{not} geometric closure), \textbf{configuration} (an upper-bound construction).
Unless stated otherwise, every route in this Part carries
\texttt{machine\_\allowbreak{}verified\_\allowbreak{}geometry = false} and \texttt{global\_\allowbreak{}optimality = false}, and the global
interval quoted by the reports of that era is $11323/100000<\rD(100)\le1154469548855829/10^{16}$.
Artifact paths are given relative to the repository root; see Part VII for which of them live in the
published mirror.

\section{Combinatorial and topological routes}

The purpose of these routes is to turn a continuous covering into a finite combinatorial object: a
planar map, a dart map, a triangulation with a fixed face catalogue, or a boundary word.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Planar / dart-map compiler.} vertices with independent edge identities, face permutations, ordered
four-axis markings; half-edge code $(\alpha,\phi)$ keeps multiple-edge identity; bounds
$V\le2m+2$, $E\le3m+1$; model budget $k_M=19m+22$ ($1922$ at $n=100$) \emph{Status: diagnostic.} Evidence: \texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}n100\_\allowbreak{}generic\_\allowbreak{}map\_\allowbreak{}compiler.py}, \texttt{n100\_\allowbreak{}dart\_\allowbreak{}map\_\allowbreak{}canonicalization.py},
\texttt{n100\_\allowbreak{}raw\_\allowbreak{}dart\_\allowbreak{}model\_\allowbreak{}adapter.py} \\
Map canonicalization
\end{itemize}


\section{Boundary-circle, axis and box-pruning routes}

This family attacks the configuration space directly: propagate interval constraints on centre
coordinates, split boxes, and count how many disks can serve the boundary.

\item \textbf{Capacity frontier.} capacity / Hall-type necessary conditions on boundary service; box-aware
pruning; the non-separated three-point capacity cut \emph{Status: partial.} Evidence: {\footnotesize\texttt{docs/\allowbreak{}result-\allowbreak{}n100-\allowbreak{}stage25-\allowbreak{}capacity-\allowbreak{}frontier.md}} \\

\end{itemize}


\section{Arc-pair, nogood and learning routes}

This family works on one fixed core and one narrowed parent domain, learns necessary relations
between boundary-tile labels, and asks how much support remains.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Arc-pair consistency and cycle support.} necessary pair relations between arc tiles sharing a centre
(vertex-box minimum distance $\le2U$); $289\,530/277\,368$ pairs reduced to $1866/2312$ labels;
exact support of the inherited finite pairwise model via an independent-set DP on the $32$-vertex
cycle \emph{Status: undecided.} Evidence: \texttt{verify/\allowbreak{}out/\allowbreak{}n100-\allowbreak{}arc-\allowbreak{}pair-\allowbreak{}consistency.json},
\texttt{n100-\allowbreak{}arc-\allowbreak{}cycle-\allowbreak{}support.json} \\
Ordered and monotone cycles
\end{itemize}
